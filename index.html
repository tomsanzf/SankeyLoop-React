Where Flows Find Their Shape: Quantitative Sankey Flow Modeling

SankeyLoop turns complex energy systems, thermodynamic heat balances, material distribution networks, and corporate financial cash flows into interactive, high-precision Sankey diagrams right inside your web browser without installing any software or sacrificing data privacy.

What is a Sankey Diagram?

A Sankey diagram is a quantitative flow visualization where the width of each connecting arrow is strictly proportional to the magnitude of the quantity it represents. Originally developed by Irish engineer Matthew Sankey in 1898 to analyze the thermal efficiency and exergy losses of steam engines, Sankey diagrams have become the foundational industry standard across mechanical engineering, chemical process design, sustainability auditing, and macroeconomic modeling.

By immediately highlighting where input streams originate, where intermediate transformations occur, and where ultimate losses or outputs end up, Sankey diagrams eliminate the ambiguity of standard double-entry spreadsheets or static bar charts.

Key Technical Applications of SankeyLoop

  • Thermodynamic Heat Balance & Exergy Destruction: In chemical refining, power generation, and industrial heating cycles, more than 50% of fuel input energy is frequently rejected as waste heat through flue gas stacks, condenser water cooling loops, or radiation. SankeyLoop introduces thermal gradient coloring to map temperature changes across enthalpy flows.
  • Corporate Cash Flow & P&L Waterfalls: CFOs and financial analysts utilize SankeyLoop to transform complex GAAP income statements into clear financial waterfalls, showing how gross revenue cascades down through cost of goods sold (COGS), research & development (R&D), marketing overhead, taxes, and EBITDA net margins.
  • Local-First Client-Side Privacy: When modeling proprietary manufacturing yield balances or confidential corporate budgets, transmitting raw numbers to third-party cloud servers poses security risks. SankeyLoop operates 100% locally within your browser using HTML5 local storage—your numbers never leave your computer.
